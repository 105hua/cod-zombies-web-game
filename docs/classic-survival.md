# Classic round-based Survival: World at War through Black Ops II

[Research index](./README.md) · [Cross-game comparison](./game-comparison.md)

**Scope and access date: 2026-09-18.** This account concerns the original console/PC versions of _World at War_ (2008), _Black Ops_ (2010), and _Black Ops II_ (2012), not mobile adaptations or later remasters. Der Riese was a 2009 expansion to World at War. The selected maps illustrate different survival economies; they do not exhaust these games. Strategic interpretations are marked **synthesis**.

## What “Survival” means here

In World at War and Black Ops, survival describes lasting through successive waves. In Black Ops II, **Survival is also a named mode**: Bus Depot, Farm, and Town are bounded arenas, not the full TranZit journey. TranZit connects locations through bus travel, exploration, and buildable equipment. Grief instead pits human teams against one another to outlast the opposition, without directly shooting opponents dead. Contemporary reporting of Treyarch’s presentation establishes these distinctions. [CS01]

There is no scheduled winning round or extraction in the standard survival matches discussed here. Clearing a wave advances the round; a team can recover from individual casualties, but incapacitation of the whole team ends the attempt. [CS02, CS03]

## Opening: turn weak equipment into spending power

The selected maps start players with an **M1911 pistol, a knife, and grenades**, rather than a chosen primary-weapon loadout. Kino’s ordinary start also provides 500 points. Players initially cover entrances, repair damaged barricades, earn points, and decide between purchasing a weapon and opening another route. [CS03–CS05, CS07, CS09, CS16, CS17]

Classic scoring rewards **damage as well as kills**. The cross-era points reference lists 10 points for a nonlethal hit, 100 for a lethal headshot, and 130 for a lethal melee strike. These are action awards, not a promise that every kill yields the same total. Repairing boards also awards limited points; it does not cost points. [CS11]

**Synthesis:** Weakening zombies with the pistol before finishing with the knife can earn more than immediately killing, but becomes dangerous as close-range pressure grows. Spending everything on random weapons can delay doors or Juggernog. The period Kino guide recommends hit/knife point-building; its particular route is advice, not a rule. [CS12]

## Midgame: establish a sustainable position

**Nacht:** Open useful rooms, obtain reliable weapons, and manage approaches to a defensible position. The Mystery Box stays in the Help room. Without perks or Pack-a-Punch, progression remains weapon-focused. Opening staircases changes access routes; the map reference discusses leaving one barricaded when camping upstairs. [CS04]

**Der Riese:** Purchased doors lead to power, perks, teleporters, and traps. Linking the teleporters unlocks Pack-a-Punch at the spawn-area mainframe; upgrading costs 5,000 points. **Synthesis:** Setup now has a substantial second stage beyond merely obtaining a good gun: fund access, improve survivability, unlock upgrading, then organize a firing position or movement route. [CS05]

**Kino:** Stage power connects the stage to the lobby, activates facilities, and introduces gas-emitting crawlers. Pack-a-Punch costs 5,000 points. Periodic Hellhound-only waves reward Max Ammo when cleared. **Synthesis:** The teleporter, circulation routes, traps, and Thundergun support movement-and-clear cycles rather than indefinite opening-window defense. [CS06, CS12]

**BOII Survival:** Bus Depot emphasizes opening its outside area and acquiring weapons. Farm adds perks but never Pack-a-Punch. Town supports upgrading, but damaging lava complicates movement and machine access. The map-specific guide describes training around obstacles: gather following zombies, create separation, shoot safely, and repeat. These are representative strategies, not mandatory solutions. [CS08–CS10]

Wall weapons offer predictable purchases and repeatable ammunition purchases; the Mystery Box trades certainty for a chance at stronger or unusual equipment. Pack-a-Punch improves a weapon only where that machine exists. These maps should not be described as sharing a universal upgrade ladder. [CS03, CS08]

## Escalation and high rounds

Zombies become harder to kill as rounds advance, and ordinary later rounds generally demand more killing and sustained movement. Enemies enter through barriers and other map-specific spawn approaches; rebuilding windows delays access rather than permanently securing an area. [CS02]

An important exception: **original World at War solo Nacht has a per-round population cap** that Der Riese does not share. Round totals must also be distinguished from simultaneously active enemies. No universal spawn equation, exact health curve, or high-round threshold is asserted here. [CS02]

**Synthesis:** High rounds increasingly test repeatable safe movement, ammunition supply, traps where available, and down recovery. Nacht and Bus Depot cannot compensate by buying Juggernog or upgrading; Farm cannot use Pack-a-Punch. [CS02, CS04, CS08, CS12]

## Health, downs, recovery, and failure

Juggernog increases survivability where offered; Speed Cola improves reload speed. Black Ops II’s Double Tap 2.0 also improves bullet damage, unlike treating the older fire-rate perk as the same effect. Exact health totals and hit counts are intentionally omitted: secondary guides contain conflicting or erroneous figures. [CS03, CS13]

In co-op, a downed player can be revived before bleeding out. If they die, they wait for the next round, provided another player survives to finish the current one. A normal revive preserves held weapons but loses purchased perks; returning after death requires rebuilding the weapon loadout. This creates a recovery problem in the middle of an already difficult match, not a reset of the wave’s difficulty. [CS03, CS14]

Quick Revive marks a major historical change: in **World at War it does not rescue a solo player**; in **Black Ops and BOII**, where available, solo Quick Revive provides automatic recovery, is consumed, and ordinarily permits three purchases. It is not present in the selected original Nacht or BOII Bus Depot. [CS15]

Town’s co-op Tombstone is a further exception: a player can give up while downed, then recover their deposited weapons and other perks after respawning and collecting their tombstone. It requires surviving teammates to reach that next round; it is not solo insurance. [CS18]
