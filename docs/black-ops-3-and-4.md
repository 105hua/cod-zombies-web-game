# Black Ops III and Black Ops 4: two different survival models

[Research index](./README.md) · [Cross-game comparison](./game-comparison.md)

**Scope and access date:** 2026-09-18. This describes representative, unmodified round-based Zombies in **Black Ops III (2015)** and **Black Ops 4 (2018)**, principally the PC/PS4/Xbox One releases. BO4's reference case is **Classic, Normal difficulty**, not Rush, Gauntlet, or Custom Mutations. Historical launch articles establish their contemporary systems, not every later balance value. Community references supply mechanical detail; this is not an exhaustive map guide.

## Black Ops III: classic purchasing plus powerful preparation

### Maps and match progression

**The Giant** provides a comparatively direct example: open routes, activate power, and link three teleporters to the starting mainframe to unlock Pack-a-Punch. **Shadows of Evil** instead integrates Beast transformations, item collection, and rituals into upgrade access. Completing that setup is not the same as completing the map's entire story quest. The Giant is the BOIII revisiting of Der Riese: its BOIII weapons and GobbleGums must not be attributed to the original World at War map. [B34-1, B34-2]

**Opening — synthesis:** preserve enough money to open the map and obtain a sustainable weapon rather than repeatedly gambling at the Mystery Box. BOIII retains points for nonlethal hits, with additional kill rewards: weakening enemies before finishing them can finance setup. Doors, ammunition, perks, and upgrades compete for the same in-match points. Juggernog remains a purchasable health increase, making reaching it a significant safety milestone; ordinary perk purchasing is generally limited to four, with reward/GobbleGum exceptions. [B34-3, B34-4]

**Midgame — synthesis:** establish upgrade access and a repeatable safe route, acquire perks, and pursue the map's useful equipment or Wonder Weapon. Buying a gun does not end its progression. **Weapon Kits** apply previously unlocked attachments and cosmetics when eligible weapons are acquired; they do not simply give the player that gun at spawn, and some added weapons lack kit support. The ordinary first Pack-a-Punch improves the weapon. Repacking an eligible weapon adds or rerolls an **alternate ammo type**, such as Dead Wire or Turned, rather than providing BO4's successive damage tiers. The Giant guide documents the conventional 5,000-point first upgrade and 2,500-point repack. [B34-1, B34-5]

**High rounds — synthesis:** survival becomes maintaining spacing, ammunition, and repeatable crowd-clearing tools as enemy health and round workload increase. Training means grouping pursuing enemies along a movement route; camping means defending a position. Which is sustainable depends on map geometry, available traps, Wonder Weapons, and alternate-ammo effects—not merely owning a high-level account weapon. Basic setup can support an extended survival run without finishing the optional story quest. [B34-5, B34-6]

### GobbleGums and recovery

A player selects a five-type **GobbleGum pack** before play, then obtains random draws from map machines. Effects range from temporary concealment to ammunition or power-up assistance. Classic gums are unlocked through progression; Mega gums use an account inventory obtained through Dr. Monty's Factory with Liquid Divinium. Taking a Mega from the machine consumes stock even if its effect is never subsequently activated. Consequently, an unrestricted consumable run and a restrained/classics-only run need not have comparable setup pressure. That last comparison is **synthesis**, not a separate official difficulty setting. Machine pricing changed after launch; the 2015 official guide should not establish a permanent price schedule. [B34-2]

In co-op, a down requires rescue before bleedout. BOIII ordinarily removes purchased perks on a down, making the recovery journey dangerous even after revival; particular gums can preserve them. In solo, Quick Revive supplies automatic recovery and must be reacquired after use, with a normal machine-purchase limit of three. These limits are not an exhaustive account of gums or map-specific rescue methods. Without a remaining recovery route, solo defeat or team incapacitation ends the survival attempt. [B34-4, B34-6, B34-7]

## Black Ops 4: the loadout becomes part of survival strategy

### Maps, preparation, and perks

**IX** represents Chaos play: its arena and connecting temple areas introduce Tigers, Gladiators, and Blightfathers alongside ordinary zombies. **Voyage of Despair** takes place aboard the Titanic and uses the Sentinel Artifact in progression. **Blood of the Dead** and **Classified** are Aether reimaginings of Mob of the Dead and Five, respectively—not those older maps with identical setup or revive rules. The shared BO4 loadout systems apply, but geography, enemies, and unlock tasks remain map-specific. [B34-8]

Before entering, choose a starting weapon from unlocked options, rechargeable equipment, a **Special Weapon**, four perks, four Elixirs, and optionally a Talisman. The Armory customizes eligible weapons. Special Weapons are already part of the loadout: their charge and in-match advancement supply powerful combat opportunities rather than requiring a random Mystery Box roll. Chaos and Aether offer different Special Weapon selections; the Scepter of Ra, for example, combines offensive and ally-support functions. [B34-8]

Perks are assigned to fixed machine/altar slots before the match and still purchased with points during it. The fourth slot receives its **modifier effect when all four selected perks are active**. Thus, the player plans both the perk combination and where its purchases occur. Juggernog is absent; health is determined by difficulty rather than bought through that familiar perk. Secret Sauce is an exception to a completely predetermined perk set because it supplies a random perk. [B34-8, B34-9]

**Elixirs** are activated directly from the equipped selection, subject to cooldowns, rather than drawn from a machine during the round. Classic Elixirs have unlimited stock; consumable rarities spend inventory. **Talismans** supply a starting advantage or a benefit lasting that match and are consumables, not permanent account stat increases. Nebulium Plasma funds Laboratory acquisition. [B34-8, B34-10]

### Economy, escalation, and a typical run

**Opening — synthesis:** use the chosen starting tools to earn points, open routes, and reach map-specific setup objectives. Unlike BOIII, BO4 does not reward indefinitely adding more weak hitmarkers: ordinary damage-point earnings are tied to portions of an enemy's health, with a bounded total and finishing bonuses for headshots/melee. Therefore a weak “point-building gun” no longer has the same economic role. This is not the later Cold War kill-only system. [B34-3]

**Midgame — synthesis:** purchase the planned perks, complete Pack-a-Punch access, and continue upgrading while maintaining ammunition and defensive equipment. BO4 allows most weapons **four additional damage-boosting Pack-a-Punch purchases after the initial upgrade**, also applying/rerolling alternate ammo. Further rerolls are not unlimited damage growth. This makes repeated investment meaningful in a way that differs from BOIII's alternate-ammo repacking. [B34-5, B34-11]

**High rounds — synthesis:** rotate powerful charged tools and map-specific weapons around a sustainable route while responding to special enemies. BO4 Casual/Normal zombie health eventually caps; Hardcore/Realistic should not inherit that assumption. More enemies and continued positioning/resource demands still make later rounds harder. The difficulty selector also changes player resilience and enemy behavior; Casual disables most quests. Launch health figures are especially unsafe to quote without version context: the October 14, 2018 balancing pass increased Classic Casual/Normal/Hardcore player health and adjusted point earning. [B34-6, B34-12]

### Downs, quests, and modes outside the baseline

BO4 separates solo self-revives from **Quick Revive**, which instead improves health recovery and teammate revival. Downs can remove perks, with remaining perks decaying during a co-op bleedout; special loadout effects can modify that loss. A prompt rescue therefore matters beyond merely preventing death. Once the solo player's available recovery is exhausted, or the team is incapacitated without rescue, the run ends. Story quests are an additional goal, not the requirement to advance every ordinary round; some quest completions can themselves end a match. [B34-4, B34-6, B34-7]

Keep these separate:

- **Rush:** a deliberately simplified, differently paced alternative—not evidence for Classic purchasing rules.
- **Gauntlet:** a 30-round sequence of specific challenges; failures accrue strikes, with three ending the attempt. Its mandated weapons or restrictions are not ordinary Survival rules.
- **Custom Mutations:** adjustable fundamental rules, including zombie speed/health and Mystery Box behavior. Results require their settings to be stated. [B34-13]
