# The flow of a typical Survival match

[Research index](./README.md) · [Mechanics](./mechanics-and-economy.md) · [Game comparison](./game-comparison.md)

This chapter is **synthesis** of the linked guides, not a claim that all players follow one strategy. “Early,” “setup,” and “high-round” describe the state of a run; they are not universal round-number brackets.

## 1. Choose the run and enter the starting area

Players choose a map and solo or co-op play. In the classic examples, the opening kit is weak and players must acquire most of their power during the match. In later games, loadout and progression choices already shape the opening: BO4 provides configurable starting equipment, while Cold War and BO6 support a selected starting firearm and Field Upgrade. See the [classic](./classic-survival.md), [BO3/BO4](./black-ops-3-and-4.md), and [modern](./modern-survival.md) chapters for the actual rules.

Players first identify spawn entrances, nearby purchases, possible escape routes, and the next useful unlock. The official BO6 Liberty Falls guide illustrates this well: spend the opening rounds around Pump and Pay, then choose between the Riverside and bowling-alley routes. Its round suggestions are guidance, not scripted objectives. [F1]

## 2. Turn the first rounds into a usable economy

The initial objective is not simply “kill everything immediately.” It is to survive while earning enough to avoid being trapped with inadequate equipment.

In classic games, repeated nonlethal hits and a melee or headshot finish can earn more than a quick ordinary kill. That creates a tradeoff between money, ammunition, and the risk of letting enemies close. BO4 and subsequent games change this incentive, so an old pistol-and-knife points routine is not a universal opening. [F2]

Early spending usually competes between:

- A dependable weapon now.
- A door that gives access to better purchases or more room.
- A survivability perk, where available.
- Saving for the first major weapon upgrade.

A Mystery Box gamble can accelerate progress or consume money without solving the immediate problem. A known wall weapon is less random. Neither is always the right purchase. This tradeoff is visible in the BOII map guide and BO6 opening guidance. [F1, F3]

## 3. Open useful space and establish map access

Players expand the playable area and unlock useful systems while the horde keeps applying pressure. On Kino der Toten, this involves reaching power and linking the teleporter to access Pack-a-Punch. On Town Survival, Pack-a-Punch is already in the central lava area; there is no equivalent power-and-teleporter setup. On Bus Depot Survival, no amount of searching unlocks a Pack-a-Punch machine that the map does not contain. [F3, F4, F5]

**Important distinction:** ordinary map setup is not necessarily the map's main story quest. Turning on power or unlocking an upgrade machine may be useful for a survival run even if the player ignores the main quest.

Purchases occur in the world rather than in a universally safe shopping phase. Players commonly leave a final enemy alive to buy or prepare before clearing the round. This is a practical delay, not a pause or a guarantee that an enemy can be preserved indefinitely; BO7's official Survival guide specifically recommends making such purchases quickly. [F6]

## 4. Build a sustainable fighting routine

Once essential access and equipment are secured, the focus shifts from opening the map to keeping a repeatable combat pattern alive.

Two recognizable approaches are:

- **Holding a position (“camping”):** defend predictable approaches while firepower keeps up. The danger is losing the position when enemies break through or ammunition runs out.
- **Kiting a horde (“training”):** keep moving so pursuers collect behind the player, create separation, then turn to fire or lead them through a trap. The danger is being intercepted by new spawns or another group.

The BOII location guide recommends specific outdoor loops. BO6's official guidance discusses holding positions, choke points, and circular escape routes; BO7 recommends using the barn's elevation to gain distance. These are map-dependent tactics, not separate selectable modes. [F1, F3, F6, F7]

During this phase players repeatedly replenish ammo, replace consumables, improve weapons, acquire perks, and prepare an emergency escape or revive tool. Modern games add armor maintenance, Salvage expenditure, and Field Upgrade charge management to that routine. [F7; modern chapter]

## 5. Adapt as pressure rises

Higher rounds generally increase combat pressure, but not through one franchise-wide formula. Enemy health, population, movement, spawn pacing, special enemies, and caps vary by title and map. Original World at War has important population exceptions; BO6's official reveal explicitly describes faster enemies and more dangerous enemy mixtures at higher rounds. [F7; classic chapter]

The practical question becomes: **can this setup still clear enemies safely and replenish what it consumes?** A weapon that felt excellent at the start may no longer clear an escape path quickly enough. A route that works against melee pursuers may fail when ranged or heavily armored enemies join them.

There is no standard final survival round at which every title awards victory. Record-chasing, completing challenges, and simply beating a previous attempt are player goals, not mandatory win conditions. [F4]

## 6. Recover from a down—or lose the run

A down is distinct from a completed death in co-op. A teammate may revive a downed player before bleed-out. If the player dies, classic co-op generally makes them wait for a subsequent round to return, with major equipment losses. Perk loss and recovery exceptions depend on the title and map. Solo substitutes limited self-recovery mechanics where available; original World at War Quick Revive is not the later solo extra-life system. [Classic chapter]

**Synthesis:** a revive creates a team-level risk decision. The rescuer may need to distract or clear enemies first; rushing directly into the same trap can turn one down into a wipe. After recovery, the player may need to rebuild defenses while enemies are already stronger than when those purchases were first made.

A run fails when no surviving player or permitted recovery mechanic can keep it going. “One person went down” and “the entire match ended” are therefore not interchangeable descriptions.

## 7. Continue, finish, or extract where supported

Classic Survival is principally an endurance run ending in defeat or the players leaving. Later round-based games add alternatives:

- **Exfil:** in Cold War and BO6, players can attempt an extraction rather than continue indefinitely. This is an optional combat challenge, not an instant safe exit.
- **Main-quest completion:** on quest-bearing maps, a separate objective path can lead to a finale. Its relationship to continuing the match is map-specific.
- **Saving a solo run:** BO6 supports save-and-quit; suspending a run is not the same as beating it. [F8; modern chapter]

For a concrete BO6 example, the official Liberty Falls guide offers exfil on round 11 after completing round 10. Players must reach the extraction area and eliminate the required enemies before time expires; failure ends the attempt. This timing must not be copied into every game or playlist. [F1]

## Three compact examples

| Example                   | Typical sequence                                                                                                                                 | What it demonstrates                                                                      |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| BOII Town Survival        | Earn points → buy a workable weapon and useful doors → buy perks and Pack-a-Punch → manage outdoor routes and lava → recover or eventually wipe. | A compact map can contain the familiar upgrade loop without a story quest or power setup. |
| BO6 Liberty Falls         | Earn Essence → open a route toward the church → weigh perks against Pack-a-Punch → maintain rarity, ammo, and armor → continue or attempt exfil. | Modern survival layers several upgrade/resource systems onto the round loop.              |
| BO7 Vandorn Farm Survival | Fight within the farm → purchase available upgrades → use the barn, ground-level routes, and traps → repeat under increasing pressure.           | Named Survival can retain modern upgrades while removing the large-map quest journey.     |

These are illustrative sequences derived from [F1, F3, F6], not prescribed round-by-round solutions.
